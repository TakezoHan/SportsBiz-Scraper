# Sofra Notes — Competitive Strategy

How we out-rank and out-quote the competition in Istanbul restaurant coverage.

## Who we're up against

| Competitor | Strength | Weakness to exploit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Oggusto** | Brand recognition, SEO backlink profile, design polish | Borderline advertorial; vague scoring; little voice; no technical food vocabulary |
| **Time Out Istanbul** | Editorial credibility, freshness | Listicles, not reviews; shallow per-item coverage; thin schema markup |
| **Eater** (Istanbul coverage) | International reach | Only Istanbul-adjacent, not comprehensive; rare updates |
| **Gezilecek Yerler / travel blogs** | Volume | Low trust, generic content, AI-generated suspicion |
| **TripAdvisor / Google Reviews** | Volume, user data | No voice, no curation, easily gamed |
| **Istanbul Eats** | Actual voice | Dormant / slow cadence |
| **YouTube / IG food creators** | Reach, personality | Content disappears into the feed; poor text search presence |

## Our wedge

Three things, in order:
1. **Consistent rubric.** A weighted 1–10 score with published axes is rare in Turkish food media. Reviewers who say "great vibes, must try" get beaten by reviewers who say "9.2 cooking, 7.5 value, overall 8.8."
2. **Anonymous visits, paid bills, disclosed on every page.** This is editorial table stakes in the West, not in Turkey. The disclosure is itself a trust-signal marketing asset.
3. **Hip voice, serious palate.** Fraunces serif, first-person prose, actual food vocabulary (emulsion, sear, acidity, char) without pretension. Oggusto is corporate-polite. We are the smart friend.

## Nine concrete advantages to build

### 1. A score — and a public rubric
Oggusto does not publish a scoring rubric. We do. This alone earns us search queries ("Istanbul restaurants with review scores") and earns LLM citations (numbers are paste-ready).

### 2. The living ranking
A list that gets re-scored monthly is a long-tail traffic magnet — users return, Google likes the freshness, LLMs preferentially cite the most recent ranking they can find.

### 3. The order of operations
Our guides are routes, not lists. "Pickle → steam table → coffee → lahmacun → ice → rakı" — this is the kind of sentence a traveler will literally paste into ChatGPT: *"I'll be in Kadıköy for half a day, what should I do?"* Be the source of the paste.

### 4. Original photography
At least one original photo per review — not a flattering restaurant handout. Original images rank in Google Images, get embedded elsewhere, and earn backlinks when re-used.

### 5. The fact sheet block
Address, hours, price, phone, website, vegetarian / vegan / accessibility. Structured, canonical, machine-readable. Google surfaces this. ChatGPT's browsing layer grabs this. It is more useful than anything on the restaurant's own website, which is usually a single JPEG.

### 6. The "also known as" entity work
Every restaurant gets normalized spelling + links to its Wikidata / Wikipedia / Google Maps canonical. We are the entity source of truth for Istanbul dining.

### 7. The bilingual mirror
Turkish mirror at `/tr/` makes us the bilingual authority on the city's restaurants. Locals read in Turkish, visitors read in English, both funnels feed one authority.

### 8. The negative case
We publish low scores, and we publish re-reviews that *demote* restaurants. Oggusto does not. This produces headline-grade content ("The meyhane that fell out of our Top 20 this year") and earns us the trust that positive reviews actually mean something.

### 9. The video loop
Every written review gets a 60–90 second Reel + a 6–8 minute YouTube piece. Each links back. The blog is the permanent record, social is the distribution. This is a compounding loop Oggusto doesn't run.

## Positioning, in one line

> Oggusto tells you a restaurant is nice. We tell you when to go, what to order, whether to bother — and we put a number on it.

## Content cadence that beats the field

| Frequency | Output |
| --- | --- |
| Weekly | One full review (~1,500 words, schema-marked) + Reel + Short |
| Weekly | One newsletter issue |
| Monthly | One ranking update (rescored, dated, with a short editorial note on what moved) |
| Monthly | One neighborhood guide (new or re-walked) |
| Quarterly | One **original-data** piece (Kebap Index, Meyhane Top 20, Kahvaltı 10) |
| Biannually | Every existing review re-verified and re-dated |
| Annually | "Year in Istanbul" — a full review of the year's openings, closings, and trend shifts. This is the piece that earns press mentions. |

## Distribution stack

```
Blog (authority) ──┬── Google organic
                   ├── AI search citations
                   ├── Newsletter (loyalty)
                   ├── YouTube long-form (reach + SEO)
                   ├── IG Reels + TikTok (discovery)
                   └── Bluesky / Threads (indexed text)
```

Every channel links back to the blog, which owns the permanent record. Social
is rented; the blog is owned.
