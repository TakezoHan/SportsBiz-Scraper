Van Kahvaltı Evi and the morning that changed Istanbul breakfast.

The Eastern spread is now a cliché in every neighborhood of the city. Cihangir's originator is still, fifteen years later, worth the queue.

8.4/10 Cihangir Breakfast Eastern Anatolian ₺₺ Moderate

By Elif Karahan · Updated 2 April 2026 · Visited anonymously, three times. Bill paid in full.

Long before every third café in Kadıköy ran a "serpme kahvaltı for two" at ₺900, Van Kahvaltı Evi was doing the actual thing — the full Eastern breakfast, with otlu peynir that really did come from Van, with kaymak dense enough to hold a spoon upright, with murtuğa cooked in a heavy pan somebody's grandmother would recognize.

What it is

A small, cheerful room on the Cihangir slope down to Tophane, still family-run, still serving the twenty-plus-plate spread that launched a genre. Don't be fooled by the imitators. The sourcing here is the story: the cheeses, the honey, the house-made kavurma — all from the family's network in Van and the surrounding villages.

What to order

  • Serpme kahvaltı — the full spread. You do not order à la carte. You order the whole table and you eat slowly.
  • Murtuğa — the buttery flour-and-egg skillet that is the single dish most worth crossing the Bosphorus for.
  • Kavut — roasted barley flour with butter and molasses. Sweet, smoky, extraordinary.
  • A second pot of demli çay. Always a second pot.
It is not a secret and never was. But "not a secret" is different from "not worth your time."

When to go

Weekdays, 08:30–10:00. Saturday between 11:00 and 13:00 is a zoo; Sunday is marginally saner. They close at 16:00 and you do not want to arrive after 14:00 — the herb cheese runs out, the bread loses its edge.

How we scored it

Cooking 8.6 · Hospitality 8.4 · Setting 7.8 · Value 8.0 · Come-back-Tuesday 9.0. Weighted to 8.4 / 10. Half a point off last year for pricing creep — the full spread has crossed ₺900 per person, which is no longer the bargain it once was. The cooking, though, has not slipped.
